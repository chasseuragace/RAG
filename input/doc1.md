## S1 — The River Path

Parang had stopped counting the trees hours ago.

At first, the jungle felt almost beautiful. Tall green canopies filtered the sunlight into scattered golden patches, and the wind carried the scent of wet earth and moss. But beauty changes shape when a person is lost.

By evening, every direction looked the same.

Parang pushed aside hanging vines and tried to remember where he had entered the forest. He had followed a narrow hunting trail earlier that morning, confident he could return before dark. Now the trail was gone, swallowed by roots, ferns, and shadows.

The sounds of the jungle grew louder as the light faded. Insects buzzed sharply. Branches cracked somewhere far away. A distant howl echoed through the trees.

Parang swallowed nervously.

“I should’ve turned back sooner,” he muttered.

He kept walking until exhaustion forced him onto a fallen log. His legs ached, and his throat was dry. The small bottle of water he carried had been empty for hours.

That was when he noticed the rabbit.

It stood beside a cluster of blue flowers a few meters away, unusually calm for a wild animal. Its fur was white except for a gray patch around one eye.

The rabbit stared directly at him.

Parang blinked. “You’re not afraid of me?”

The rabbit twitched its nose once, then hopped away a short distance before stopping again.

Parang watched carefully.

“You want me to follow you?”

The rabbit hopped forward again.

Parang laughed quietly despite his situation. “Well, Bingo. That’s your name now.”

Bingo led him through the jungle in a winding path. Sometimes the rabbit disappeared behind bushes, only to reappear moments later, waiting patiently. Parang followed because he had nothing else left to trust.

The forest slowly changed around them. The ground became softer and damp beneath his boots. The air felt cooler.

Then Parang heard it.

Water.

At first it was faint, hidden beneath the noise of insects and leaves. But with every step, the sound grew clearer until the trees finally opened into a wide riverbank reflecting the last orange light of sunset.

Parang rushed toward the river and knelt beside it, drinking deeply.

Relief washed over him.

He knew this river.

The villagers near his hometown often spoke about it. The river curved along the edge of the jungle and eventually passed near the main road. If he followed it downstream, he could make it home.

Parang turned toward Bingo with a grateful smile.

“You saved me.”

The rabbit sat quietly near the rocks, ears raised.

For a moment, neither moved. The jungle no longer felt hostile. The fear that had wrapped around Parang all afternoon loosened like fog disappearing in sunlight.

“Come with me,” Parang said softly.

But Bingo simply looked toward the forest.

Then, with one final twitch of its nose, the rabbit hopped back into the trees and vanished among the shadows.

Parang stood there for a while, listening to the river.

After that, he began walking downstream toward home, guided by the sound of flowing water and the memory of a rabbit named Bingo.
